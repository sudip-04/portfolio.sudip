01 / 07

Emerging
design identity

My journey into design began long before formal tertiary education. In my 9th year of school, crafting a personal study table introduced me to scale, ergonomics, and structural joinery. Crafting a physical scale model of a local Buddha Air airplane in Nepal further deepened my appreciation for aerodynamics, precision, and hand craft.

Through my current studies at the University of Canberra, my core understanding of design has evolved from purely aesthetic making to an integrated process connecting physical structure, digital modeling, and real-world construction delivery. Moving from hands-on physical physical models to analyzing large-scale structures—such as the Westfield Belconnen carpark and the Qantas Maintenance Hangar at Canberra Airport—has shifted my perspective toward load paths, spatial geometry, and material performance.

Compared to my initial views in Assessment 1, where I viewed design primarily as drafting visuals, I now see design as a problem-solving dialogue between intent, technical constraint, and execution. I am currently most curious about how digital modeling tools like AutoCAD and Revit bridges the gap between conceptual architecture and physical construction.

03 / 07

Discipline
direction

My core focus lies at the intersection of Building Construction Management, Architectural Modeling, and Structural Analysis.

My discipline path is shaped by a dual interest in structural design logic and digital execution. Experiencing both physical fabrication—such as my Belconnen-inspired truss bridge—and digital drafting with AutoCAD has shown me that effective design requires both creative intuition and rigorous technical discipline.

Pursuing Building Construction Management allows me to bridge the space between design concepts and structural execution. While architectural visualization drives my interest in Revit skyscraper design, understanding how structures support real-world loads keeps my path grounded in construction management and structural efficiency.

Maintaining a multi-disciplinary viewpoint that connects construction management, structural engineering, and digital BIM workflows ensures I can coordinate effectively across clients, engineers, and construction teams on site.
